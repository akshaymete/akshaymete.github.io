Research

Training objectives for deep world models

Optimistic World Models change how a world model is trained: an optimistic dynamics loss, derived from reward-biased maximum likelihood estimation in adaptive control, biases imagined transitions toward higher-reward outcomes. The loss is fully gradient-based, needs no uncertainty estimates, and plugs into existing world-model architectures; we instantiate it in DreamerV3 and STORM.

Key paper: arXiv 2026

Reward-biased model learning

Reward-Biased Maximum Likelihood Estimation (RBMLE) fits a model of the environment by maximum likelihood, biased toward models that promise higher reward, so that the model the agent learns is shaped by what it needs to control. I have developed RBMLE for Markov decision processes and linear systems, with applications to safe, multi-objective, and RLHF settings.

Key papers: L4DC 2021 · Allerton 2023

Learning models in closed loop, with guarantees

For linear systems, I study how well an agent can learn its model while controlling with it, measured by regret. PIECE is a self-tuning regulator for auto-regressive systems with the first O(log T) regret bound for self-tuning regulation, and augmented RBMLE-UCB brings reward-biased model learning to adaptive linear–quadratic control.

Key papers: ICML 2024 · NeurIPS 2022
